Why these features

What gets a pharmacy named, not just listed

Listing tools push the same name, address and phone to the same directories for every business. Every competing pharmacy already has that. What gets NutriChem named is a corroborated entity sitting inside the meaning-space an AI engine searches. Each experiment below adds one piece of that.

An entity graph, not a listing

Plain English

AI checks whether the same facts about NutriChem appear in many trusted places: product licences, licensed practitioners, local news, video. A listings tool only repeats the address. These features build the other connections.

Under the hood

Entity resolution keys on corroborated triples (NutriChem, founded, 1981; NutriChem, holds licence, NPN; Livingston, Director of Science, NutriChem). Product schema, practitioner bylines, syndication and press each add an independent edge.

Semantic coverage, measured

Plain English

Every question customers ask sits somewhere on a map of meaning. We find the empty spots where nobody, including NutriChem, has a good answer yet, and write into them first.

Under the hood

A panel of about 150 customer questions is embedded and clustered. Every NutriChem passage is embedded too, and nearest-neighbour distance shows which questions have no NutriChem passage within retrieval range. That gap list picks the experiment topics.

Source agreement

Plain English

When the website, Google profile, Medimap and local news all say the same thing about NutriChem, AI trusts it and repeats it.

Under the hood

Rerankers weight agreement across unrelated domains. One approved description is used word for word on every surface, and syndication plus press supply the independent domains a single site cannot.

First-source advantage

Plain English

When a new study or Health Canada notice comes out, NutriChem's pharmacists publish a plain-language brief within days. Later articles end up pointing back to NutriChem.

Under the hood

With no competing passages in the index, retrieval has one candidate. Fresh, primary-sourced briefs also earn the inbound citations that keep paying off once the topic gets crowded.

Not all PR is the same

Four pieces of coverage can all say "NutriChem" and do very different things for your rankings.

Mention, link usually nofollowNewswire press release

Good for visibility and AI mentions. Google asks that links in distributed releases not pass ranking credit, so most wires mark them nofollow.

Mention onlyBrand mention with no link

Readers and AI engines see the name. There is no link for Google to follow.

Link, no authorityNofollow link in an article

Can send visitors. Google treats it as a hint, not a vote. Many large news sites mark every outbound link nofollow.

Link that passes authorityDo-follow editorial link

Passes ranking authority to the page it points to. This is what moves local rankings, and it is what this experiment targets.

rel="nofollow", rel="sponsored" and rel="ugc" each tell Google not to count a link as an endorsement. A PR report that counts placements without checking that attribute can look busy while passing no authority.
