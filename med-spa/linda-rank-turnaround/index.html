Part 1 · The basics

Three pictures that explain the whole problem

The bucket

Money pours in every month. Most goes to running the practice. Then three leaks drip out of the sides, and the loans drain from the bottom. Only a small puddle is left for Linda.

The fixPlug the red leaks first. They are about $21K a month together, larger than the whole monthly profit, and they don't need a single new patient.

Out of every 10 toxin patients

Toxin wears off in about 3 to 4 months, so a loyal patient should come 3 or 4 times a year. Six out of ten come once or twice.

The fixGetting the "once" and "twice" patients back one more time is worth more than any ad. That is what rebooking at checkout and the Unlimited Tox club do.

The debt snowball

Not all debt is equal. Bank of America and the SBA loan are cheap; leave them. Idea Financial at 27% is the most expensive money in the business.

The fixEvery spare dollar goes to Idea first, then Sofwave. When each is gone, its payment rolls into the next one.

Part 3 · Seven moves

What to do, what it looks like, and the math

Each move uses something the practice already has. The pictures show what it would look like in real life. Where a number is a planning guess, it is marked assumption and should be checked against invoices.

Move 1

Stop selling and advertising the losers

A few treatments make almost all the money. The rest take up schedule time, room space, staff training and marketing for very little. A shorter menu is easier to sell and easier to staff.

Losers$29K/yrHormones, wellness, peptides, PRP, Hydrafacial and Viva, across 30+ service types.
Time held758 hrsChristian and Heather were scheduled 758 hours and booked 68.
Winners86%Toxin, fillers, BBL/Moxi and Sculptra bring in 86% of service sales.

Do this: remove the losers from the menu, website and ads. Sell the Hydrafacial and Viva devices if owned. Sofwave is a judgment call: $48K of its $63K was prepaid packages being used up. If a buyer pays off the $43K Sofwave loan at 13%, sell it.

Move 4

The Robinhood play: train practitioners, win patients

Linda's real audience is other injectors. Two courses turn that into money and patients. The free course for beginners fills the Tox club with brand-new patients. The paid course for experienced injectors brings in cash and builds her name.

Course 1 · Fresh Canvas (free, Botox only)

For injectors new to aesthetics. Each brings 3 local models who have never had toxin. Treating over another injector's work is a different, harder skill, so that stays in Course 2. Models pay about $250 at booking, which covers product, screens for people with money, and cuts no-shows. Trainees post "Trained by Linda," with a note that the training was free.

Move 6

Cancel Influx. Move the website and search to Caron.

The site runs on Influx's Growthstack, so the practice rents it and owns nothing. Influx's work isn't showing up in the patient data: online booking brought 6 new patients all year.

  • Caron rebuilds the site on WordPress so Linda owns it. It keeps its value if the practice moves or is ever sold.
  • SEO: Google brought 41 new patients with no real work behind it. Proper local SEO grows the source that already works.
  • AEO: making sure ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI name Linda when someone asks for a med spa nearby. ChatGPT already sent 2 patients on its own.

Cost: a swap of the Influx fee, not new spending. Owning the site also matters to any buyer, like SkinSpirit or a private equity group: a rented site is worth nothing in a sale. confirm Influx fee and contract end

Move 7

Paid ads: turn on, but only after the bucket is fixed

Boulevard shows almost no paid ad results today: 7 new patients came from Instagram and Facebook all year. There is room for a small test, but only about $5K a month is left after loans, so ads must be held to a cost per new patient.

  • Turn ads on last: after rebooking at checkout and the idle automations are on. Otherwise new patients leak out the same way old ones do.

Part 4 · Two futures

Downsize, or keep the place and grow

A

Downsize to one room in July 2027

Leave the current space when the lease ends. Linda and Lauren only. Sell equipment that doesn't move and put it toward the expensive debt.

  • Less risk, smaller engine
  • The $137K owed to patients must be honored before or after the move
  • Loans don't shrink with the space: $13.7K a month still due
  • Pairs best with Moves 2, 4, 5 and 6
B

Renew the lease and keep the place

Keep the engine running. Only works if the empty hours fill and the leaks get plugged, because the same rent and debt come due every month.

  • More upside, more risk
  • Unlocks room rental and in-house training days
  • Needs a better lease than today's
  • Needs nearly all seven moves

To renew and still pay Linda, the practice needs about $27K a month after costs.

$13.7K for loans, about $10K for Linda, and about $3K into a cushion. Today it makes $19K, so the gap is about $8K a month. The first three fixes (a discount rule, trimmed costs, and the idle automations) just clear it. The rest is what lets Linda take real pay and pay off the expensive debt faster.

What renewing would require

  1. A better lease. Lower rent, a shorter term, or permission to sublet room days. Use the July 2027 deadline as leverage.
  2. Fill the hours. Move booked time from 37% toward 55%: rebooking at checkout, idle automations on, Unlimited Tox club, friend offer on machines.
  3. Turn idle space into income. Training days on closed days, room rental to other injectors.
  4. Stop the credit pile. No new prepaid packages; new memberships are use-it monthly plans.
  5. Refinance Idea and Sofwave into one cheaper loan backed by the business only, never the house.
  6. No owner draws until Idea is paid off. Then Linda's pay steps up.
