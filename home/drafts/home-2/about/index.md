Chapter one

## How finding a business changed.

For twenty years, finding a business meant *searching and clicking.*

You typed a few words, opened tab after tab, compared reviews and called around.

Now people just ask. And AI gives *one answer.*

No list of links. No tabs. One recommendation, said with confidence.

But AI can only recommend what it *understands.*

And for most local businesses, the hours, prices, reviews and licenses online don't agree with each other.

And now everyone is *building walls.*

Websites are locking AI out, charging tolls or suing each other. Every new wall makes the answers a little worse.

1. *2023***Publishers take AI to court.**The New York Times sues OpenAI and Microsoft over its articles.
2. *2024***Data goes behind deals.**Reddit licenses its posts to Google and OpenAI.
3. *2025***The web blocks AI by default.**Cloudflare starts blocking AI crawlers unless they pay per crawl.
4. *2025***Stores fight shopping agents.**Amazon sues Perplexity to stop its AI agent from shopping for people.

So AI guesses. Or it picks *someone else.*

Often the biggest name, not the best one. A great small business can be invisible without ever knowing why.

AI is deciding who to trust *right now.*

When everyone builds walls, the small business disappears.

Chapter two

## So we're doing the opposite.

We keep the door *open to everyone.*

Every AI assistant, agent and crawler can read public business facts for free. No walls, no tolls. Private information stays private.

One record for every business, with *a source on every fact.*

Confirmed by the owner or an official source, added by a local, or still an AI guess waiting to be checked. Every fact says which.
