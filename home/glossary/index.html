Local AI search glossary

Plain definitions of the terms you'll meet when you work on getting your business recommended by AI. Each one says what the term means, why it matters for a local business and what to do about it.

AEO (answer engine optimization)

Writing and structuring content so assistants and search features can lift a direct answer from it.

AEO puts the answer first: a clear two-sentence response to the question, followed by detail, an FAQ and schema markup. It overlaps with GEO; AEO is about how your pages are written, GEO is about the wider set of sources AI trusts.

Related: GEO (generative engine optimization), AI Overviews, Schema markup

AI Overviews

The AI-written summaries Google shows above some search results.

AI Overviews summarize an answer and link to sources. For local questions they draw on Google's search index and local business data. Google's AI Mode is a separate, conversational search built on the same data. See how to show up in Google's AI answers.

Related: AEO (answer engine optimization), Entity

AI Score

Local AI Registry's measure of how ready a business is to be recommended by AI, based on the 11 signals.

The AI Score looks at all 11 signals AI uses to decide which local business to recommend and summarizes them in one number, with the detail behind it. Our free AI visibility check starts with it.

Related: AI visibility, AI share of voice, Local AI Blueprint™

AI SEO

A broad term for search optimization that accounts for AI answers as well as traditional rankings.

People use AI SEO to mean anything from using AI tools to write content to optimizing for AI assistants. On this site we mean the second: making sure AI assistants recommend your business. See GEO and AEO.

Related: GEO (generative engine optimization), AEO (answer engine optimization)

AI share of voice

The share of relevant AI answers that name your business compared with competitors.

If you ask 20 customer questions across several assistants and you are named in 6 of the answers, that is your share of voice for that question set. Tracking it monthly is the core of AI monitoring.

Related: AI Score, AI visibility

AI visibility

How often, and how well, AI assistants mention and recommend a business when people ask questions it could answer.

AI visibility is to assistants what search rankings are to Google. A business with strong AI visibility is named when someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini or Google's AI answers for the kind of service it offers, and it is described accurately. It depends on eleven signals, from consistent listings to reviews.

Related: GEO (generative engine optimization), AEO (answer engine optimization), AI Score

Citation

A mention of your business on another website, or a source an AI assistant links to in its answer.

The word has two uses. In local SEO, a citation is any listing or page that mentions your business details. In AI answers, a citation is a source the assistant shows to back up what it said. Being cited by AI usually starts with being cited around the web.

Related: NAP consistency, GEO (generative engine optimization)

Entity

A distinct, identifiable thing, such as a business, person or place, that search engines and AI track as one object with known facts.

To an assistant, your business is an entity with a name, location, category, services, people and reviews. The clearer and more consistent those facts are across the web, the more confidently AI can talk about you. Schema markup and NAP consistency are how you make your entity clear.

Related: Knowledge graph, NAP consistency, Structured data

GEO (generative engine optimization)

The practice of improving how a business or brand appears in answers written by AI assistants.

GEO focuses on the sources AI reads and cites: reviews, articles, lists, structured data and your own pages. For a local business, GEO means making sure every assistant can confirm who you are, what you do and why you're a good choice. See how it compares with traditional SEO in AI visibility vs SEO.

Related: AEO (answer engine optimization), AI SEO, Citation

Knowledge graph

A database of entities and how they connect, used by search engines and AI to understand facts.

Google's Knowledge Graph and similar systems link your business to its location, category, people and other facts. A strong presence in these graphs makes it easier for AI to recognize and describe you.

Related: Entity, Structured data

llms.txt

A proposed plain-text file at the root of a website that points AI systems to its most useful pages.

llms.txt is a community proposal, not an official standard, and assistants vary in whether they use it. It's quick to add and does no harm. Make one with our llms.txt generator.

Related: Structured data

Related: AI Score, AI visibility

NAP consistency

Having the same business name, address and phone number everywhere your business is listed.

NAP stands for name, address, phone. Many practitioners add hours. Inconsistent NAP makes it harder for AI to confirm which listings belong to you. See directory listings.

Related: Entity, Citation

Schema markup

Code added to a web page that labels facts, such as your business name, hours, services and reviews, in a standard vocabulary.

Schema markup uses the shared vocabulary at schema.org, usually written as JSON-LD. It doesn't change how a page looks; it tells machines exactly what the page is about. Build yours with our free schema generator.

Related: Structured data, Entity

Structured data

Information organized in a fixed format machines can read without interpretation.

Schema markup is the most common kind of structured data on websites. Business listings, menus and inventory feeds are others. The more of your key facts exist as structured data, the less AI has to guess.

Related: Schema markup, Knowledge graph
