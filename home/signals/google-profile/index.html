Does my Google Business Profile affect AI recommendations?

By Steve Lee, founder of Local AI Registry
Updated November 16, 2026
Short answerYes, heavily for Google's own AI answers and Gemini, which draw on Google's local data, and indirectly for other assistants that read Google results. A complete, accurate and active profile is one of the highest-value fixes for any local business.
Book a free AI checkSee client results

Why it matters beyond Google Maps

Your Google Business Profile is the most widely read record of your business on the internet. Google's AI answers and Gemini lean on it directly. Other assistants often see it through search results. When it's thin or out of date, every assistant gets a weaker picture of you.

What we do for you

We write the posts on your Google Business Profile and send each one to you before it publishes.

A post is drafted from things we hold about you: the services on your listing, the searches you picked, your photos and your brand rules. Nothing is written from a template.

Every draft is checked against our writing standard, then by an automatic check that blocks the usual mistakes: the wrong town, a made-up statistic or a medical claim.

You choose the cadence. Daily, a few times a week, weekly, or paused.

What you get

  • A draft in your approvals queue with the question it is aimed at named on it.
  • An email with the draft and the date it publishes if you leave it alone.
  • Edit, approve or decline. A declined draft is not published and the next one takes a different angle.
  • The published post on your listing, with the date and the text kept on your account.

How it runs

  1. A slot comes due Your cadence decides when. A post drafted inside the same window is not drafted again, so a weekly cadence is a post a week and not a post a day.
  2. The draft is written and checked Written from your own facts, then checked and rewritten up to three times until it passes. A draft that still fails on the third pass never reaches your queue, and the next slot tries a different angle.
  3. You see it first The draft lands in your queue and an email goes to you with a publish date three business days out. If it sits unread, one reminder follows.
  4. It publishes On approval, or on the date in its email if you leave it alone. Decline it and it doesn't go up. You can set posts to wait for your yes in your settings.

Why it moves the AI answerAn assistant answering a local question reads the Google listing, and a listing that has said nothing in four months reads as a business that may not be trading.

Complete every field

  • The right primary category, plus every secondary category that fits.
  • Every service, each with a short description.
  • Hours, including holiday hours.
  • Photos of the outside, inside, team and work, labeled clearly.
  • Questions and answers filled in by you.

Keep it active

Post an update every week: a service, an offer, an event, a tip. Reply to every review. Add new photos monthly. Activity tells Google, and the assistants that read Google, that the business is open and cared for. It works together with reviews and photo labeling.

Which assistants lean on it most

Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Gemini put the most weight on this signal, based on how each one finds local information. Every assistant uses it to some degree.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I post?

Weekly is a good rhythm. It keeps the profile current without becoming a burden.

What's the most common mistake?

The wrong primary category. It shapes which searches and questions you appear for, so check it first.

Do posts go out without me seeing them?

Every post comes to your inbox first with the date it goes live. Leave it and it publishes that day, decline it and it doesn't. If you'd rather approve each one yourself, set posts to ask you first in your settings.

Can I change the wording?

Yes. Edit it in the queue and the edited text is what publishes. You can also decline a draft, and declining does not cost you the slot.

What stops a post making a medical claim?

A scan runs on every draft and on the title. An outcome promise, a recovery time, a typical result or a safety word blocks the draft outright for health-adjacent categories.
