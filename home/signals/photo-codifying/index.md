**Short answer** Partly. AI can now describe images, but it reads the text around a photo more reliably: the file name, alt text, caption and the page it sits on. Labeling photos clearly turns them from decoration into evidence of what you do.

## Why labels matter

A photo named IMG\_4821.jpg tells a machine nothing. The same photo named and described as "dental implant consultation room, Irvine" tells it what the room is for and where it is. Multiply that across every photo on your site and profile, and AI gets a much clearer picture of your services and space.

## What we do for you

We collect your photos, write the words that travel with each one, and publish them onto your Google listing a few at a time.

Pictures come from three places: your Google listing, your website, and any social profile we hold a link to. Only public pages, and a private one is stepped past.

Two sets of words are written per photo. One is embedded in the file itself, in the fields Google and every modern library read. The other is the description published under the picture on your listing.

Both use only the words on your own service list and what kind of picture it is. We never let AI write a description of a medical image, and before and after photos are never described at all.

- Files carrying your business, your service and your location in their own metadata.

### How it runs

1. **Photos are collected** From the listing, the website and your social links, once per business. You can upload more at any time.
2. **Each one is classified** Against the services on your own record, so the words we attach are a service you actually offer.
3. **The words are written in** Each file gets a title, description, keywords, your city and a credit. We don't describe what's in the picture.
4. **They publish on a drip** One a day per business. Google limits how fast new photos can go up, and a pile posted in one day looks like spam.

**Why it moves the AI answer** A photo with no words attached is invisible to anything reading rather than looking, and the description under it is the part a retrieval system can quote.

## How to codify a photo

1. Rename the file with plain words for what it shows.
2. Write alt text in one sentence: what is in the picture and why it matters.
3. Add a caption on the page where it helps the reader.
4. Place the photo on the page about that service, not in a generic gallery.
