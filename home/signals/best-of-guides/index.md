**Short answer** Often, yes. When someone asks an assistant for "the best" business of a type in a city, it frequently reads local best-of lists and guides to answer. Businesses that appear on several of those lists are much more likely to be named.

## Why lists carry so much weight

A best-of list is already an answer to the exact question the customer asked. It's easy for an assistant to read three lists, see which businesses appear on most of them and recommend those. If you're on none, you're relying on other signals to overcome that.

## Find the lists that matter

1. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity for the best business like yours in your city.
2. Look at the sources they cite.
3. Search Google for "best [your service] in [your city]" and note every list on the first two pages.
4. That set of lists is your target.

## Get onto them honestly

Many lists are written by local publications and bloggers who welcome good tips. Introduce yourself, explain what makes you different and offer facts they can check. Some lists are paid placements; know which ones before you spend. Strong [reviews](../../signals/reputation/index.html) make every pitch easier.
