## What the AI Score measures

The AI Score is a single number, from 0 to 100, that measures how findable and recommendable a local business is to AI assistants like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google’s AI Overviews. It is the metric we built Local AI Registry around, and we publish exactly how it is calculated. A high score means AI can find you, trust you, and name you when a customer asks; a low score means it cannot.

The number matters because the results are stark. Across the local businesses we have scored, nearly two in three land below 45 out of 100, and barely one in seventy-five clears 65. Most local businesses are not close to being recommendable by AI, and most owners have no idea where they stand.

## The eight signals behind the score

The AI Score is not a black box or a single-metric gimmick. It is built from eight signals, each measuring a different part of whether an assistant can find and recommend you. Every weight is documented in our [scoring methodology](../../methodology/index.html).

- Retrieval grounding: can AI find and cite you for the queries that drive bookings, not just your brand name.
- Off-property attestation: what independent sources across the web say about you.
- AI visibility: whether specific assistants already name you today.
- Reviews: volume, recency, rating, and whether you respond to them.
- Google Business Profile: how complete and accurate your listing is.
- Authority: the quality of the sites that link to and reference you.
- Speed and mobile: whether your site is technically eligible to be cited.
- Content and schema: whether your pages are clear and machine-readable.

The first two signals, retrieval grounding and off-property attestation, carry the most weight, because they are what actually decide an AI recommendation. The other six are eligibility: necessary, but not sufficient.

## How the number is calculated

Each signal is scored on its own, then combined into the final 0-to-100 number using a weighted geometric mean rather than a simple average. The distinction matters. A geometric mean leans on your weakest signal, so a business cannot paper over a missing off-property presence with a fast website. If any load-bearing signal is near zero, the overall score stays low, which mirrors how an assistant actually behaves: one strong attribute does not earn a recommendation when a critical one is absent.

The weights come from how much each signal moves a real AI recommendation, not from what is easiest to measure. That is why two businesses with identical websites can score very differently: the one the wider web corroborates scores higher, because corroboration is what the assistant grounds its answer in.
