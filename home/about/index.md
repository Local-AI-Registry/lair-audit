Most local businesses are in pieces online.

Their  hours,  prices,  reviews and  licenses live on dozens of sites. Many of them disagree.

And now everyone is building walls.

Websites are locking AI out, charging tolls, or suing each other. Every new wall makes the answers AI gives a little worse.

1. 2023**Publishers take AI to court.** The New York Times sues OpenAI and Microsoft over its articles.
2. 2024**Data goes behind deals.** Reddit licenses its posts to Google and OpenAI. Most of the web gets no deal at all.
3. 2025**The web blocks AI by default.** Cloudflare starts blocking AI crawlers for new sites unless they pay per crawl.
4. 2025**Stores fight shopping agents.** Amazon sues Perplexity to stop its AI agent from shopping for people.

So AI guesses. Or it picks someone else.

Often the biggest name, not the best one. A great small business can be invisible without ever knowing why.

AI is deciding who to trust right now.

When everyone builds walls, the small business disappears.

### How Often AI Recommends Your Business: A Better Measure of Local Visibility

Local AI Registry Research · 14 references · 9 peer-reviewed

**Abstract.** A single AI answer is an observation, not a ranking. Across studies of web search, generative search, local audits and medical recommendations, answers to the same question vary from run to run, by place, by wording and over time. We argue that local visibility is best measured as a recommendation frequency under stated conditions, and propose five principles for measuring it.

**Fig. 1** Evidence that answers behave like samples. Each bar is its own metric; values are not comparable across studies.

Businesses "always present" across locations, ChatGPT local searches**3.5%** BrightLocal, 200k local AI searches

Median overlap, same question asked ~39 minutes apart**29%** 8ight.AI, Study 01

Doctors matching a real clinician: without search vs with search**10.8% → 63.9%** Ibrahim and Zaki, 2026 (preprint)

Appropriate recommendations, depending only on how the question was worded**46.6% vs 74.7%** Parikh et al., Cureus 2024

**Fig. 2** One question, asked 20 times. Blue = named. The rate on the right is what we should measure, not any single answer. *Illustration with simulated data.*

**Fig. 3** How sure can you be? 95% interval for a business named 30% of the time, by number of answers sampled. One answer tells you almost nothing. *Illustrative calculation.*

Five principles for measuring AI visibility

1. Report a rate, with its conditions
2. Sample enough answers
3. Separate being named from being first
4. Verify what is counted
5. Tell noise from real change
